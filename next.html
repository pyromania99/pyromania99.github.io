What I haven’t shown yet

Each of these has a specific test planned. They are my next experiments.

On hardware

  • The adaptive lag compensation has not flown. On hardware its measurement signal does not yet match the lag it is meant to cancel, so it needs a better-filtered signal first.
  • The estimator fix is untested on hardware above 15.7 rad/s. The vehicle has flown just below it.

On the larger claim

  • Motor-loss survival is open. In simulation no spin rate survives a single rotor loss with an allocator that isn’t told; It is left to see if the wings will save it in simulation and hardware.
  • No head-to-head yet against the leading published method for lag compensation on a spinning quadrotor.
  • Lift and power rest on a few short trials

Where I’m taking this

The pattern in my work is the same one I want to apply next: choose the mechanism and the control law with each other in mind.

A universal limb

Ground robots whose limbs do both jobs, locomotion and manipulation, inspired in part by how an octopus arm grips objects of any size. Household tasks such as dishes, laundry or fetching something from the fridge are the target. Most of the field is heading toward humanoids; I want to come at it from the limb.

Interpretable learned control

I’m adding learned locomotion to my toolkit, with one aim: combine learning with my control background so the learned controller stays nonlinear and interpretable, and I can still reason about it.
