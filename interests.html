Robot

MOBIUS

A four-limbed bipedal robot from UCLA’s RoMeLa and MERL, by Yusuke Tanaka, Dennis Hong and colleagues: two arms for manipulation and climbing, two legs for locomotion. It switches between walking, crawling, climbing and rolling without reconfiguring, combining reinforcement learning for locomotion with force control for contact-heavy manipulation. Tanaka’s wider work is on contact-rich, simultaneous locomotion and grasping and on limbed climbing.

Fingers as Legs

Kazemipour, Zheng & Katzschmann, 2026. An anthropomorphic hand that walks on its own fingers: it crawls, steers and recovers from falls untethered, and while carrying its weight it still types keyboard commands and pushes objects to targets, with no separate locomotion mechanism.

For my limb: one limb reused for locomotion and manipulation is exactly the question I start from.

Design

Geometry for strength

Childs & Rucker, 2021. A 3D-printed, rhombus-patterned backbone with converging tendon routing that raises a continuum robot’s load capacity sharply while it can still bend.

For my limb: geometry can supply a stiffening floor, and its deflection depends on how it is loaded, which is the assumption that breaks existing stability proofs.

Material

Lattice musculoskeletal robots

Guan, Dai, Cheng & Hughes, 2025. One printed material spans tissue-like compliance to bone-like load bearing purely through lattice geometry, programmed cell by cell.

For my limb: geometry-only stiffness of this kind is what I want the design search to explore.

Analysis

Active softening

Peyron & Burgner-Kahrs, 2024. A stability analysis of tendon-driven continuum robots, showing that tendon actuation can soften a backbone toward buckling.

For my limb: stiffness becomes an input-dependent quantity, an active knob on top of the passive floor that geometry provides.

Read the paper ↗

Proof

Stable control of a soft arm

Della Santina, Katzschmann, Bicchi & Rus, 2020. Model-based control of a planar soft arm with a Lyapunov stability proof that does not cancel its softness.

For my limb: the proof assumes a constant stiffness. Reproducing it, and finding where a load-dependent stiffness breaks it, is my first step.

Read the paper ↗

Control

Low-stiffness soft robots

Nair, Caradonna, Mathew, Renda, Della Santina & Feliu-Talegon, 2026. Real-time nonlinear MPC on a variable-strain model stabilises soft robots at unstable equilibria, where gravity dominates elasticity.

For my limb: control guarantees for soft robots hold only above a stiffness threshold, and a limb that stiffens under load crosses it as its payload changes.

Read the paper ↗

Control

Variable impedance stability

Kronander & Billard, 2016. Stability conditions for a robot whose controller varies its stiffness and damping over time.

For my limb: this covers stiffness raised for load by the controller; stiffness set by the load itself is the case I have not found analysed.

Learning

Stable by construction

Khansari-Zadeh & Billard, 2011 (SEDS). Learns a dynamical-systems controller from demonstrations under constraints that guarantee stability.

For my limb: a certified controller class like this lets a design search explore only designs whose controllers can be certified.
