Five areas I can go deep on. Tags show how the work was validated: Hardware Simulation Analysis Published

Classical control design

HardwareAnalysis
  • Design and tuning of cascaded position, attitude and rate loops, using pole placement and gain scheduling, for a vehicle with strong gyroscopic coupling.
  • Showed why a standard PD cascade cannot hold a fast spin, and designed a feedback-linearising controller that cancels the coupling. It flies on hardware.
  • Frequency-domain analysis of actuator delay and motor lag. On a spinning body a delay rotates the command direction, so phase error, not lost gain, is what limits the loop.

Lyapunov and backstepping controllers

AnalysisSimulation
  • Stability proofs for a full position, attitude and rate cascade on SO(3), without small-angle approximation.
  • Backstepping design for the thrust direction, with the cross terms between stages cancelling by construction.
  • An adaptive law that compensates actuator lag without needing to know the lag, with a stability argument, tested in simulation. I also checked which effects my own proofs do not cover.
  • The Lyapunov and backstepping designs are analysis only; the controller I flew uses feedback linearisation.

Disturbance analysis

AnalysisSimulation
  • Classify disturbances by whether they rotate with the vehicle (centre-of-gravity offset, rotor mismatch) or stay fixed in the world (wind), and derive their effect in closed form. Results matched a full nonlinear simulation.
  • Analysed a single rotor failure as a disturbance: the authority that remains and the steady coning angle the vehicle settles into.
  • Identified when a disturbance cannot be told apart from a command by filtering, and what that means for adaptive control.

PX4 firmware and edge-style architecture

HardwareSimulation
  • The whole controller runs as a module on the flight controller, with no companion computer. I chose this over the usual route for lower weight, cost and power, and so anyone already flying PX4 can use it.
  • Found and fixed a bug in PX4’s attitude estimator that appears at high rotation rates, and added a regression test.
  • Custom control allocation, pilot modes that work while the vehicle spins, and rotor-failure injection.
  • High-rate flight logging, timing and latency analysis, and PX4 simulation in lockstep with Isaac Sim.

Hardware design, development and prototyping

HardwarePublished
  • Designed and built the quadrotor airframe in two configurations, with 3D-printed motor mounts and a carbon-fibre structure.
  • Sized wings for low-Reynolds-number flow using blade-element theory, CFD and XFLR5, with a custom airfoil. Built them and flew them.
  • Tuned the spin rate mechanically by tilting motors. A simple model predicted the next build’s spin (15.2 rad/s predicted, 14.6–15.5 flown).
  • Instrumentation: motion capture, an external-vision relay, rotor-speed telemetry and a spin test rig.
  • Tools: Fusion 360, PCB design, 3D printing. Also the mechanical structure for a CanSat that placed 4th worldwide at design review.
